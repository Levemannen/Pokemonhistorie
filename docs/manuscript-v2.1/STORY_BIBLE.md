# Story Bible — The Mystery of the Lost Starlight · v2.1

## Dramatic question
Can a child convince a frightened, well-meaning friend to return what she borrowed without abandoning the person she was protecting?

## Five acts
1. **The Night the Sky Went Quiet:** The festival collapses into darkness; Mira, the emergency seed and a concrete map make the journey necessary. The child and starter form a bond before leaving.
2. **Moonwood Holds Its Breath:** Ordinary small kindnesses have immediate consequences. The lens reveals an incomplete memory and rules for safe light transfer; the hero learns that borrowed light must return.
3. **The River Without Reflections:** Competing routes converge on the same island memory. Evidence of an injured Shinx and a frightened Lumi changes the question from “Who stole the stars?” to “What frightened her enough to do it?”
4. **Above the Sleeping Clouds:** The journey has plausible crossings, optional mountain detours, a three-way observatory challenge and a coherent tower entrance. The approach choices change Lumi’s reception.
5. **A Light to Bring Home:** Lumi explains the accident; Shinx speaks and gives consent; the child discovers three constrained solutions and makes a genuinely consequential choice among six outcomes.

## Place and time
Willowbrook sits in a valley below Moonwood. A ridge and brook are alternate routes into the forest. Moonwood slopes to Lumen River. Ferry, Lapras crossing and waterfall all reach a single river island. The northern island path climbs for roughly an hour to a bridge below the mountain observatory. The observatory adjoins Starfall Tower via a short covered walkway; old steps and a service tunnel provide alternative approaches. The festival begins just after sunset; the mountain is reached before dawn. Early dawn breaks at the climax.

## Rules of the light
1. The actual stars never leave the universe. The Glass intercepts the reflected starlight that normally reaches *this valley only*. Outside valleys retain their sky.
2. The old mechanism was designed for short emergency heat and lighting needs. Keeping its return path locked removes reflected light from Willowbrook and destabilizes the mechanism.
3. The Glass can close when a single frightened keeper refuses to transfer responsibility. This lock is mechanical-magical, not an arbitrary judgment of who is good.
4. The silver seed is a living receiver and emergency key, released when the lock persists. Its glow is strongest near a compatible caretaker. It cannot create unlimited energy.
5. The crescent lens focuses the light of the seed and reveals stardust memories only on surfaces recently marked by light. The island is marked; the lens cannot see the future or anything it wishes.
6. SKY returns all reflected light immediately. Before release, Shinx must have safe transport and non-Glass warmth.
7. HEART temporarily distributes warmth among willing helpers or lanterns while releasing all star reflections. No one is compelled or harmed.
8. EARTH transfers *warmth*, not stars, to a maintained living receiver. The trapped reflections return fully to the actual sky in this ending too.
9. The three-note song is a rhythm used to coordinate and soothe; it is not unexplained healing magic.
10. All paths include at least one viable ending even if the player missed optional friends, clues and items.

## Characters
- **Reader:** A young visitor entrusted with lighting a festival lantern. Curious, kind, courageous or playful according to choices; never treated as already knowledgeable.
- **Mira:** Village lantern keeper and practical caregiver; knows old diagrams, once cut the dangerous tower location from her public map, and now accompanies the quest through signals, equipment and the promised safe welcome. Always introduced in the prologue.
- **Lumi (Clefairy):** Warm, clever, overly responsible, fearful of losing Shinx and of being blamed. She activated the Glass and then hid, but had already sent a cry for help. She apologizes and helps carry out the chosen repair.
- **Shinx:** Fiercely independent, proud, hurt after a storm fall and cold because of unstable electrical charge. Wants to be asked what happens to him; funny, stubborn, important to all final decisions. A bandage is not enough by itself; rest, ordinary warmth and continued care matter.
- **Bulbasaur:** Patient, practical, protective of small growing things; helps with vines, roots and gentle repair.
- **Charmander:** Eager but deliberately careful about fire; helps with warmth, safe illumination and frozen gears.
- **Squirtle:** Organized, safety-minded, mischievous; helps with signs, water channels and counterweights.
- **Eevee:** An initially frightened optional companion who helps show Lumi what calm welcome looks like; otherwise stays with Mira.
- **Hoothoot:** Keeper of the three-note song. Travels only when explicitly asked; otherwise guards nest and teaches via memory.
- **Pikachu:** Trapped by ivy responding to light. May travel, guard the forest or share information. Must never pop up as a traveler unless recruited.
- **Lapras:** River guide, not a mountain hiking companion. Can promise a crossing and appear as a distant song, never physically stand on the mountain.
- **Psyduck:** Nervous, persistent ferry worker. Traveling is voluntary; workshop projects have actual later payoff.
- **Altaria:** Flock watcher and mountain shelter keeper; supplies passage and a warm feather after credible cooperation.
- **Gastly:** Mischievous archive keeper whose humor becomes helpful rather than cruel; optional lore shortcuts require an actual conversation.

## Six endings and central resolution
- **endDawn / The Homeward Dawn:** Immediate return; Shinx receives village care and Lumi accepts responsibility.
- **endAurora / The Aurora Promise:** Safe mountain warmth; all stars return, with brief auroral afterglow from excess light.
- **endSong / The River of Stars:** Familiar rhythm coordinates release; song is reassurance, not unexplained healing.
- **endSanctuary / The Starlight Sanctuary:** Safe maintained warmth stays in a staffed tower facility; all trapped reflections go home immediately.
- **endLantern / The Lantern League:** The village cooperatively carries temporary warmth; the sky is immediately restored.
- **endGarden / The Living Constellation:** A maintained living receiver stores local warmth; the original stars return to the sky.

## Setup → payoff chain
- Mira and her map → routes and welcome home.
- Missing piece → Mira explains why she removed it, rather than magic erasing a random district.
- Silver seed → emergency signal, response to help, earth-ring receiver, or planted in final afterword.
- Three-note song → waterfall, calming rhythms and song ending if actually learned.
- Hoothoot nest / marked danger → immediate feedback; Mira receives marked message by lamp signal.
- Letter → Lumi's plea for help; response travels by stream/intake or Hoothoot.
- Crescent lens → sees *recent* island memory and identifies mechanism.
- Yellow scarves → two different objects: Lumi wears hers; Shinx lost his; returned one changes Shinx's confidence.
- Three rings → explained in archive/cave as optional foresight, and in full by Lumi as a mandatory final reveal.

## Narration convention
Pokémon vocalizations are represented as translated meaning in a storybook narrative; humans can interpret tone and gesture. No unexplained universal human speech is required. The story keeps all playable text in English.

## V2.1 continuity decisions
- On routes that do not rescue Pikachu, the reader signals Mira’s forest helpers. Their arrival is confirmed in the clearing, without granting the reader credit for the rescue.
- A baseline heated carrier and a safe path down are always prepared before the SKY ring opens. Music calms the group but does not replace a heat source.
- HEART's release latch is held until willing helpers or lanterns have taken up the necessary warmth. Mira answers the tower signal before the lantern-league method.
- EARTH stores a limited safe amount of warmth in the living receiver; the player may then carry it to Willowbrook after the stars are already returned. Its village planting does not occur simultaneously with the tower release.
- Starter-specific prologue and ending paragraphs are conditional, so a reader only sees the Pokémon they chose.
